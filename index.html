Dataset: Vision Zero Report Card Dataset

One dataset that caught my attention was the Vision Zero Report Card Dataset https://github.com/MarvinBregiosa/vision-zero-fars. This dataset focuses on truck related fatalities across 19 major US cities, 2018 through 2024. What interested me most about this dataset, among the many provided, was how a large collection of crash data can be analyzed and turned into information that has the potential to improve road safety. This dataset in particular was used as a public evaluation tool comparing truck related fatalities across major cities, rather than just data sitting on a spreadsheet. The potential a dataset like this has in helping city planners, transportation departments, researchers, policymakers, and road engineers in making cities and roads safer is very high. The way these users can utilize this dataset is by comparing cities and/or states, identifying locations with higher fatality rates, studying how the fatalities change over time, and evaluating whether the road safety programs implemented are improving the road safety conditions. Overall, a dataset like this has immense value in influencing better road design and future transportation policies.

Book: The Data Journalism Handbook: How Journalists Can Use Data to Improve the News

The free data science book I chose to explore was The Data Journalism Handbook: How Journalists Can Use Data to Improve the News by Jonathan Gray, Liliana Bounegru, and Lucy Chambers https://s3.eu-central-1.amazonaws.com/datajournalismcom/handbooks/The-Data-Journalism-Handbook-1.pdf, which was developed from a workshop at MozFest 2011 in London and became a collaborative project. What first drew my focus was the idea of taking raw data and transforming it into information that can tell a story while also being easily understood by non-professionals. Going back to the dataset from the previous question, Vision Zero is a great real world example of this concept, taking data and transforming it into something valuable. It takes thousands of individual crash records and transforms them into comparisons, trends, and an interactive report that tells a meaningful story about road safety. Large data in itself does not mean much. It becomes valuable when someone analyzes it, discovers patterns, and presents those patterns in a way non-professionals can understand.
